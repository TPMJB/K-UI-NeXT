K-UI G1 Bridge Rev A - first controller schematic
Controller sheet: 6 October 2026 | Review notes updated: 9 October 2026
Design stage 0.3 | Requires KiCad 10 or newer

OPEN IT
1. Extract the entire ZIP into one folder.
2. In KiCad, use File > Open Existing Project and select
   KUI-G1-Bridge-RevA.kicad_pro.
3. Open KUI-G1-Bridge-RevA.kicad_sch in the Schematic Editor.
4. Find U1 (RP2350B), U3 (boot flash), X1 (12 MHz crystal),
   and the USB / SWD / BOOTSEL / RUN connections.

WHAT THIS FIRST SHEET DOES
It adapts Raspberry Pi's official RP2350B minimal circuit and applies
our proposed GPIO allocation. G0_DD0 means GPIO0 assigned to ATA data
bit 0; it is not Dreamcast connector position 0. Every G0-G47 label is
a controller-side signal. The accompanying CSV describes the assignments.

The optional secondary flash circuit has been removed to release GPIO0.
The dedicated QSPI boot flash remains. The SD card holds games; the
separate boot flash holds the controller firmware.

The controller/CSV allocation is the original MCU-only candidate. A later
review proposes an FPGA ATA front end and optional QMI PSRAM. Neither is
implemented here. PSRAM can share the boot flash clock/data wiring with a
separate chip select; it is not a replacement for the retained boot flash.

J2 and J3 are retained reference/bench GPIO breakouts. They are NOT a
Dreamcast G1 connector, a CF connector, or an approved solder-pad map.
Do not wire them directly to the Dreamcast bus. Shared data and response
signals still need the isolation/buffer circuits listed below.

HARDWARE TARGET
Dreamcast VA1 with original 3.3 V GD-ROM retained as ATA Device 0.
RP2350B, A4 procurement target, fixed as ATA Device 1 (slave).
Native four-bit microSD interface and a socketed XIAO ESP32-C5.
K-UI owns the filesystem; the bridge supplies raw storage sectors.
Attachment: soldered G1 tap on the VA1 mainboard. Preferred mounting is
above the mainboard near IC501 (BIOS) and CN503, below the installed
upper metal shield. The preferred IC501 tap carrier has a cutout around the
existing BIOS, with independent supports. The owner confirms all CN503 pins
are accessible and basically flush with the motherboard. Prefer a second
cutout/notch, with an accessible edge/underside landing or thin local tap.
Ordinary top-side carrier pads would be above these tails; qualify the solder
cross-section, spacing and alignment before choosing a direct rigid joint.
Use CN503 for the full G1 bus; BIOS-leg taps remain optional for BIOS features.
A thin underside tap with a short flex to the top-side board is a fallback;
the complete board is not assumed to fit underneath. Initial clearances are
owner-reported; complete assembly fit remains unverified.
Optional dual BIOS is in the architecture scope but absent from this sheet.
The alternative IC501 leg-tap candidate supplies 20 ATA nets in the traced
schematic; eight controls need other taps. Continuity, termination and solder
geometry remain unverified. The cutout avoids stacking the board above IC501.
Drive PCB identity is not a prerequisite for this mainboard attachment.

NEXT SCHEMATIC PASS
1. G1 data buffers and response isolation: data, ready, IRQ and DMA
   outputs must release the bus whenever the bridge cannot respond.
   Hardware must force isolation during startup, reset and power failure.
   Preserve ATA data hold and final-word DMA ownership; see the review
   disposition before adopting a combinational output-enable gate.
   Evaluate the FPGA candidate and define the MCU link/pin allocation before
   selecting a front end; do not transfer the GPIO CSV to that design.
2. Native microSD socket, pull-ups, decoupling and card detect.
3. XIAO C5 socket, SPI/handshakes, separately switched supply and
   programming/USB power isolation. Its 3V3_OUT is not a supply input
   to join to the controller's 3.3 V rail.
   Qualify SPI/IRQ/READY behavior while C5 power is off, too.
4. Console supply input, early power-fail detection and hold-up supply
   for the controller plus SD card. Capacitor size awaits measured
   card write/flush behavior. The radio stays outside the hold-up load.
5. Physical G1 attachment and mounting geometry after checking the
   VA1 mainboard pad orientation, continuity and upper-shield clearances.

POWER REQUIREMENTS FOR THE NEXT PASS
The proposed inputs are labelled 3V3_IN, 5V_IN and GND. A reviewed
assembly option will select the controller/SD power path: console 3.3 V
through isolation, or console 5 V through a 3.3 V regulator and isolation.
Do not put a 3.3 V input through the retained 5 V bench regulator.
Do not electrically join the console's 3.3 V and 5 V rails.

The socketed C5 will receive a separately switched 5 V supply. One valid
configuration therefore uses 3.3 V for the controller/SD and an additional
5 V wire for Wi-Fi. Another uses a 5 V feed for the entire board, with
on-board 3.3 V regulation for the controller/SD. Neither requires another
PSU: the original Dreamcast PSU also provides a 5 V DC rail. Robot Retro
only makes the user's chosen 5 V connection more convenient.

To operate the complete board from 3.3 V alone, the C5 branch needs a
qualified 3.3-to-5 V boost stage; that is an optional implementation path,
not a working capability of this first sheet. Seeed labels the module's
3V3 pin as 3V3_OUT; it must not be used as our power-input shortcut.

Power input voltage and G1 signal voltage are separate. This VA1 design
uses 3.3 V G1 signalling even when board power comes from 5 V.
USB-to-console back-feed prevention and power-fail isolation remain
required. A 5 V wire is a power source, not stored energy for flushing SD.
Radio power will shut off on power failure; controller/SD reserve power
will be designed and sized from measurements. Available PSU headroom
must be qualified with SD writes and Wi-Fi transmit bursts together.

POWER AND VALIDATION BOUNDARY
The retained USB-powered reference supply is a bench starting point.
It is not the finished console/reserve-power circuit. No connection
between USB and Dreamcast supplies is approved by this starter.

This is an editable first sheet, not a complete board or fabrication
release. No PCB layout, Gerbers, production BOM, ATA firmware or tested
power-loss protection is supplied. Official reference component values
and footprints still require review against the chosen production parts.
Current validation results are in CHECKS.txt.

PHOTOS AND MEASUREMENTS
The owner's top-side photo identifies VA1 board 837-13778-02, IC501 and
CN503. The owner later confirmed all connector pins are accessible on both
rows. Both A/B rows carry required storage signals. Their logical mapping is
in ../docs/CN503-reference.csv.
It is not a measured solder-pad or mechanical attachment drawing.

With the console unplugged and upper metal shield seated at its normal
installed height, map motherboard PCB surface to nearest metal underside
across the candidate footprint, including existing components and wire exits.
Owner-reported IC501 dimensions: 26.68 mm body length, 12.65 mm black-body
width, 16.66 mm outer pin span, and roughly 2 mm height. Add edge/alignment
allowance to a clearance cutout; a body-only opening needs designed lead
landing geometry if it overlaps the pins. Do not infer pin pitch from these.
CN503 tails are owner-reported basically flush; precise joint geometry still
needs qualification. Obtain CN503 housing/tail envelope and X/Y position
relative to IC501, and map the lower metal and CPU/GPU thermal-contact area.
Other chips are reported significantly below 2 mm, but parts under solid
carrier areas still require height allowance or local cutouts. BIOS-top gap
matters only for a later overhang.
See the concept-outline and fabrication measurement checklist in
../docs/controller-ram-bios-disposition-2026-10-09.md.
The owner reaffirmed approximate 8.62 mm, 0.62 mm sheet thickness and 1.4 mm
additional metal near BIOS/G1 after checking the caliper readings. Use 8.0 mm
generally and 6.6 mm beneath the extra metal as owner-reported planning gaps.
The calculation assumes PCB top to shield outer face and actual downward
projection of the extra metal. The separately measured 1.62 mm motherboard
thickness is not subtracted from a PCB-top gap. Bracket overlap, cutout
dimensions and full carrier/socket/module stack remain to be checked.
The C5 is owner-measured at 4.48 mm total height including USB. After its
module envelope, 3.52 mm remains in the 8.0 mm region and 2.12 mm in the
6.6 mm region for carrier elevation, PCB, socket separation and allowance.
Keep USB in the baseline. Prefer C5 placement in the 8.0 mm region if its
footprint permits it; qualify a low-height removable connection. USB removal
is an optional fallback and needs a new measurement of the remaining module.
See the clearance and mounting budget in
../docs/controller-ram-bios-disposition-2026-10-09.md.
Do not infer clearance from internet
DCDigital figures or assume a socketed C5 fits because a BIOS chip fits.
Keep the external antenna clear of the surrounding metal shielding; its
actual location and RF performance need qualification.
